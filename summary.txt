World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026: Two days of US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi with agreement on the largest prisoner exchange of the war, at least 1,000 on each side, but no ceasefire or deal on territory (Reuters). Ukraine rejected demands to surrender all of Donbas; US envoys head to Moscow on Thursday to meet Vladimir Putin. Russia hit six power substations and a market in Chornomorsk, killing three, as Ukraine struck an oil terminal at Ust-Luga. At COP31 in Antalya, world leaders opened a two-day summit, UN chief Antonio Guterres warning the world was on a path to destruction (Reuters). Turkey pressed for electricity to reach 35% of global energy use by 2035; the US stayed away for a second year. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said pressure on Iran was working and major combat was no longer needed, but force remained possible if Iran crossed 'certain red lines' (the New York Times). The Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots in the US and Europe, including one at RAF Fairford. Tens of thousands joined Spain's first general strike in 14 years over housing and living costs, with clashes in Barcelona and transport and healthcare disrupted (the BBC). In Gaza, floods swamped about 70,000 tents, with nearly a million displaced people in flood-risk areas (Channel 4); Israeli strikes killed at least seven, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said. US stocks rebounded, the Nasdaq up 1.6%, after Tuesday's hot inflation data pushed Federal Reserve officials to float a half-point December rate rise (Investopedia). House Republicans blocked an impeachment bid against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, and British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer with a NATO unit on suspicion of spying for Russia (the BBC). Twelve people died in two pile-ups in dense fog on Bulgaria's Trakia motorway (the BBC).
